# MagicPaper Kids — Blueprint Audit (Phase 1)

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Scope:** Narrative architecture of the existing blueprint and content corpus — V1 offline blueprints, the V2 shared content library, and the runtime that assembles them into a book.
**Discipline:** This is a *narrative* audit, not a schema audit. `CONTENT_AUDIT.md` already covers bundling, decoding, and resource ownership correctly and is not repeated here. `STORY_SYSTEM_V2_PLAN.md` already covers the engineering architecture. What neither document does is ask the question this audit asks:

> **Do these blueprints describe narrative patterns, or are they single stories in disguise?**

**Nothing was modified.** All findings below are measurements taken from the checked-in content.

---

## 0. Executive finding

The system currently has **no blueprints.**

It has twelve records named `blueprint_adventure_courage_001` … `_012` which are, after their ID string, **byte-identical to one another**. Each contains: a genre, a theme, an age range, a page count, and the same ordered list of eight role names. There is no premise, no emotional arc, no dramatic mechanism, no curiosity mechanism, no conflict mechanism, no resolution mechanism, no state, no compatibility requirement, no title pattern, and no safety ceiling.

What the product calls a blueprint is a **page-count declaration**. Twelve copies of it.

The narrative content that *does* exist lives one layer below, in 52 role-scoped templates, and it is thinner than its file size suggests. The 156 template "variants" in the V2 library collapse to **27 unique sentences**. Four of the eight story roles — including the two most important, *attempt* and *emotional ending* — are served by **exactly one sentence each**, and it is **the same sentence**:

> `{{child.name}} ve {{companion.name}} birlikte çok eğlendi.`

That single line occupies 100% of `attempt` slots, 100% of `emotional_ending` slots, 83% of `companion` slots and 83% of `problem` slots. In a typical generated 8-page book it appears on **page 5 and page 8 always**, and on pages 3 and 4 about 83% of the time each — roughly **3.7 of 8 pages are the same sentence**, and ~69% of books contain it four times.

The honest summary is this: the corpus can currently produce **2,592 distinct text permutations and exactly one story.**

Every book ever generated by this system tells the following: *a child goes to a place; a companion appears; the companion appears again; it is too dark; they had fun together; an obstacle appeared; everything worked out; they had fun together.*

The user's three choices — the entire product promise — are inert. Details in §4.

This is not a content-volume problem that more writing fixes. It is an **architectural** problem: the layer that is supposed to carry narrative identity is empty, so the layer below it is forced to carry meaning it was never designed to hold, and fails.

## 1. What the measurements show

### 1.1 Blueprint layer: zero information

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Blueprint records | 12 |
| Distinct field values across all 12, excluding `id` | **0** |
| Genre / theme / age / pageCount | `adventure` / `courage` / `5-6` / `8` — identical in all 12 |
| Page-role sequence | identical in all 12 |
| Narrative fields (premise, arc, mechanism, beat, state, safety, title) | **absent from the schema entirely** |

Twelve identical records are not twelve blueprints. They are one record with an inflated count, and the count is what selection, novelty and read-history all operate on. The read-history novelty system records which blueprint the child has already seen and avoids repeating it — but since all twelve are the same, **novelty tracking is a no-op that reports success.** This is worse than having no novelty system, because it produces a green light.

### 1.2 Template layer: 156 slots, 27 sentences

| Role | Variant slots | Unique sentences |
|---|---:|---:|
| hook | 15 | 6 |
| discovery | 15 | 3 |
| companion | 18 | 4 |
| problem | 18 | 4 |
| **attempt** | 15 | **1** |
| challenge | 15 | 3 |
| resolution | 15 | 3 |
| **emotional_ending** | 15 | **1** |
| celebration *(unreachable)* | 15 | 1 |
| bedtime_transition *(unreachable)* | 15 | 1 |
| **Total** | **156** | **27** |

`visualScene` is worse: **156 slots, 19 unique strings**, most of them the placeholder `"{{child.name}} hook sahnesi."` — a field label, not a scene.

The "5 templates × 3 variants" structure is combinatorial theatre. `template_hook_1` through `template_hook_4` are four different shuffles of the same three sentences, some repeating a sentence twice *within the same template*. This creates the appearance of a variant pool to any counting tool while providing no variety to a reader.

### 1.3 The roles do not do what they are named

Reading the actual prose against the role names:

- **`discovery` does not introduce a discovery.** All three of its sentences introduce the *companion* (`"aniden karşısına {{companion.name}} çıktı"`). Page 2 and page 3 therefore both introduce the companion — the second introduction of a character the reader already met.
- **`problem` is not a problem, it is one specific problem.** All three real sentences are darkness and companion-fear: `"Yolun sonu zifiri karanlıktı"`, `"Önümüzü göremiyoruz"`. There is one conflict in the entire library, and it is hard-coupled to a lighting condition that is incoherent underwater, in space, on a sunny meadow, or in a garden at noon — all of which are selectable settings.
- **`attempt` contains no attempt.** Its only sentence is the filler. The page where the child is supposed to *act* is the page where nothing happens.
- **`challenge` is contentless assertion.** `"Tam o sırada, önlerinde kocaman bir engel belirdi."` — an obstacle appeared. Which obstacle is never stated, so it can never be overcome specifically.
- **`resolution` is unearned by construction.** `"Sonunda her şey yoluna girdi ve {{child.name}} çok mutlu oldu."` — *in the end everything worked out.* This is the exact failure the brief names at §18. Nothing caused it.
- **`emotional_ending` is the filler sentence.** No closure, no change, no reflection.

Roles 2–8 are labels applied to prose that does not perform them. The eight-role spine is sound; **nothing is currently mapped onto it.**

### 1.4 Child agency: effectively zero

Across all 27 unique sentences, the child is the grammatical subject of an *active narrative choice* in **none** of them. The child walks, looks, and is happy. The verbs of agency — noticing, deciding, trying, helping, solving, cooperating — belong to no one, or to the companion.

Worse, the companion is not a supporter but the story's **emotional centre**: it is the one who is lost, who is frightened, who asks for help. The child is a bystander to the companion's feelings. This inverts the brief's §9 requirement and inverts the product promise: the book is about the fox, and the child's name is printed on it.

### 1.5 Companion personality is authored and then discarded

The library defines fifteen companions with genuinely well-differentiated traits: `Bilge` the wise owl, `Yonca` the gentle-but-timid rabbit, `Cesur` the loyal and brave dog, `Zip` the logical-but-baffled robot, `Kıvılcım` the eager-but-clumsy dragon. This is good character work.

`personality` is never read by the generator, and no template varies by it. Therefore **Bilge the wise owl and Cesur the brave dog are both too frightened of the dark to continue** — in identical words. The single best content asset in the repository is authored, bundled, and ignored.

### 1.6 The state graph is unreachable by construction

| Direction | Finding |
|---|---|
| Challenges *require* | 15 distinct `problem=*` conditions + `failed_attempt=true` |
| Events *produce* | 7 `problem=*` conditions |
| **Unproducible conditions** | **8 of 15** — `darkness`, `high`, `lost`, `reading`, `stuck`, `waiting`, `water`, plus `failed_attempt` |

53% of challenges can never be selected: no producer exists for their precondition. And the one condition the prose actually depicts — `problem=darkness` — is required by `challenge_darkness_001` *and* by an event, but **produced by nothing**. The single narrative the library tells is the one its own state model cannot express.

This is moot in practice, because **templates carry no state fields at all** (`id`, `requiredSlots`, `text`, `visualScene` — nothing else). The state layer is disconnected from the prose layer at both ends: pages cannot read state and cannot write it. `resolutions` is an empty array. `events`, `challenges` and `endings` are never selected.

Counted exactly: of 160 content records, **66 (41%) can never reach a page** — 45 never-selected events/challenges/endings, 10 templates for the roles `celebration` and `bedtime_transition` that no blueprint requests, and 11 redundant blueprint duplicates. Nothing reports this.

## 2. Why the previous attempt produced weak blueprints

The failure is not carelessness. It is a **layering error**, and it is worth naming precisely because the same error will recur under a new schema if it is not understood.

### 2.1 The blueprint was modelled as a container instead of a contract

The V2 blueprint answers *"how many pages and in what order?"* It never answers *"what kind of story is this?"* Once the blueprint holds only shape, narrative identity has nowhere to live. It gets pushed down into templates — but templates are selected **per page, independently, at random**, with no memory of the pages around them. A layer that is sampled without context cannot hold a story, because a story is precisely the *dependency between pages*.

That is the mechanism behind every symptom in §1: page 2 re-introduces a character page 3 will introduce, page 7 resolves a conflict page 6 never specified, page 8 repeats page 5 verbatim. These are not authoring mistakes. **They are the guaranteed output of context-free per-page sampling.** No amount of additional prose fixes it.

### 2.2 The corpus was grown by duplication rather than differentiation

The growth pattern is visible in the IDs: `_001` through `_012`, identical. Four hook templates that are permutations of three sentences. Five `attempt` templates that are five copies of one sentence. The library was expanded along the axis that a counter can see (record count) rather than the axis a reader can feel (distinctness).

This produces a specific and dangerous failure mode: **every quantitative health check passes.** Twelve blueprints. Fifty-two templates. One hundred fifty-six variants. Twenty events. Fifteen challenges. Read-history novelty enabled. Compatibility metadata present. A dashboard built on these numbers would show a rich, healthy content system. The reader gets the same sentence four times.

Any V2 QA must therefore count **unique realised outputs**, never records. §6.5 proposes the metrics.

### 2.3 V1 and V2 each solved half the problem, and the halves were never joined

| | V1 offline blueprints | V2 shared library |
|---|---|---|
| Prose quality | **Strong** — 288 unique variants, ~23 words each in the best files, sensory detail, real agency verbs, earned resolutions | Weak — 27 unique sentences, filler-dominated |
| Page intent | **Present** — `narrativePurpose` on every page in the five best files | Absent |
| Reusability | **None** — setting, companion species and object are baked into the sentences | **Good intent** — tags, compatibility, novelty weights |
| Title support | **Present** — 4 `titlePatterns` per blueprint | Absent |
| Safety metadata | Present but ad-hoc, inconsistent shape per file | Absent |

V1 wrote real stories and could not reuse them. V2 built a reusable frame and put nothing in it. **The correct move is neither — it is to harvest V1's writing into a blueprint layer that V2 never built.** See §5.

### 2.4 "Different setting" was mistaken for "different story"

This is the §25 test, and the existing corpus fails it — including the good V1 files.

The five `adventure_courage_*` blueprints look diverse: a cave, a bridge, a map, a garden, footprints. Strip the nouns and read the dramatic mechanism:

| File | Challenge-page mechanism |
|---|---|
| `cave_001` | Child *remembers* something seen earlier → insight |
| `bridge_002` | Child *gets an idea* (counting song) → insight |
| `footprints_005` | Child *realises* the trail can be followed backward → insight |
| `garden_004` | Child *thinks of* using a leaf as a scoop → insight |
| `map_003` | Child *decides to trust* the owl's knowledge → cooperation |

Four of the five are the same mechanism. Four of five open that page with a near-identical sentence: `"Sonra, {{child.name}}'nin aklına harika bir fikir geldi!"`

So the strongest content in the repository contains **two dramatic mechanisms across five blueprints**, not five. The diversity is decorative — it lives in the nouns. This is the single most important lesson for Phase 2: *variety of setting is not variety of story*, and the taxonomy in §6 is built specifically to make that confusion impossible to repeat.

## 3. Page role vs. story beat — the confusion is structural, not cosmetic

The repository contains **three mutually incompatible page-role vocabularies**:

1. **Functional** (5 files + all 12 V2 blueprints) — `hook · discovery · companion · problem · attempt · challenge · resolution · emotional_ending`
2. **Screenwriting** (`underwater_*`, `space_*`, `hero_*`) — `exposition · inciting_incident · rising_action · complication · climax · falling_action`
3. **Literal plot** (`fantasy_magic_*`) — `exploration · meeting_companion · finding_object · journey · returning_object · celebration · returning_home`

Vocabulary 3 is not a role vocabulary at all. `finding_object` and `returning_object` are **beats** — things that happen — promoted into structural slots. Once a beat becomes a slot, the blueprint can only ever tell the story in which that beat occurs, and the object must exist, must be findable, and must be returnable. The blueprint has been welded to its content. This is the §5 confusion made load-bearing.

Vocabulary 1 is correct and should be the standard. It is already used by the five best files and all twelve V2 records, so standardising costs nothing.

But adopting the vocabulary is not enough — that is what the current system did, and §1.3 shows the roles are named correctly and performed incorrectly. **The role says what the page is for; the blueprint must additionally say what happens on it.** The missing field is a required story beat per page, expressed semantically (see §6.4). Its absence is why `attempt` contains no attempt.

## 4. The three user choices are currently inert

The product promise is *"I chose a few things and MagicPaper made a story for my child."* Traced end to end:

| Choice | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Place** | `StoryWizardView.swift:532` — any non-surprise selection sends `"setting_forest_001"`. Choosing *Dağ* yields the forest. |
| **Companion** | `StoryWizardView.swift:533` — any non-surprise selection sends `"companion_fox_001"`. Choosing *Baykuş* yields the fox. |
| **Secret / Discovery** | Not wired to content. An object **is** selected and bound to `{{object.name}}` — but `{{object.name}}` appears **zero times** in V2 template text. The discovery is chosen, resolved, and discarded before rendering. |

The emotionally central choice — *what will we discover today?* — has no representation anywhere in the generated book.

Two further runtime findings with narrative consequences:

- **Titles are hardcoded.** `let title = "\(request.childName) ve \(selectedCompanion.name)"` → *"Yeliz ve Kıpır"*, for every book, forever. V1's `titlePatterns` exist and are never read. §20 of the brief is entirely unmet.
- **Generation is not reproducible.** The seed is `UInt64.random(in:)` and is never persisted, nor are the selected content IDs. A book cannot be regenerated, audited, or explained after the fact.

These are engineering defects, but they are listed here because they define what the blueprint layer must own: **if the blueprint does not declare title patterns and does not bind the discovery into required beats, no downstream fix will surface either one.**

## 5. What is worth keeping

Deliberately separated from the criticism, because most of it is genuinely good and the brief's §22–23 instruction to harvest rather than restart is correct.

**Keep and promote:**

1. **The 288 unique V1 variants**, especially the five `adventure_courage_*` files (~23 words per variant, sensory, well-paced, natural Turkish). This is real children's writing and the most valuable asset in the repository. It needs decoupling, not rewriting.
2. **`narrativePurpose` per page** (V1). This is the missing story-beat field in embryo — already authored for 40 pages. It is currently in English and should become a structured, Turkish-authored beat requirement.
3. **`titlePatterns`** (V1). Four per blueprint, real children's-book shapes: *"{{child.name}} ve Parlayan Mağara"*, *"Kristal Odasına Cesur Yolculuk"*. The foundation for §20's title families.
4. **The eight functional page roles.** Correct, already near-universal.
5. **The fifteen companions with differentiated personalities.** Excellent character work, currently unused. Personality should become a *selection constraint* — a timid rabbit and a wise owl must not play the same narrative function.
6. **V2's decoupling intent** — tags, `compatibleThemes`, `compatibleSettings`, `noveltyWeight`, `cooldownWeight`. The right ideas, unimplemented.
7. **The `TurkishSuffixResolver` vowel-harmony core.** Correct for the cases it implements, and the right architectural instinct for a Turkish product (see §7 for its gaps).
8. **`adventure_01.json`'s safety and validation schema** — by far the strongest safety metadata in the repository and the correct normalisation target.

**Discard:**

- All twelve V2 blueprint records (they carry no information).
- The filler sentence and every slot it occupies.
- The 137 placeholder `visualScene` strings.
- Role vocabularies 2 and 3 (§3).
- `{{child.pronoun}}` / `{{child.possessive}}` — see §8.

## 6. Proposed blueprint taxonomy

This is the Phase 1 deliverable that feeds Phase 2. It is a **proposal for architecture review**, not the schema itself.

### 6.1 The distinctness test

The brief's §25 asks whether a blueprint is genuinely different with the nouns removed. To make that testable rather than intuitive, every blueprint is characterised on three axes:

| Axis | Question |
|---|---|
| **Curiosity mechanism** | What *pulls* the child forward — why do they not simply go home? |
| **Conflict mechanism** | What *resists* — and is the resistance external, internal, or relational? |
| **Resolution mechanism** | What does the child *do* that works — and why does it work? |

> **Distinctness rule: two blueprints are distinct only if they differ on at least two of the three axes.**

Applied to the current corpus: the five `adventure_courage_*` blueprints differ on **zero** axes from one another (curiosity: an anomaly noticed; conflict: an external obstacle; resolution: insight) except `map_003`, which differs on **one**. All five would be rejected. This is the correct result, and it is why the rule is worth adopting.

### 6.2 Six narrative families

Families are organised by the **source of narrative motion** — the most load-bearing property, and the one that cannot be changed by swapping content. Names are internal.

| # | Family | Source of motion | Conflict locus | Emotional signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **THE UNEXPLAINED** | Something is present that should not be; the child must *understand* it | External / cognitive | wonder → puzzlement → insight → satisfaction |
| **II** | **THE THRESHOLD** | Something must be entered or crossed; the obstacle is the child's own hesitation | **Internal** | hesitation → resolve → effort → pride |
| **III** | **THE APPEAL** | Someone needs something the child can give | **Relational** | sympathy → commitment → difficulty → warmth |
| **IV** | **THE ABSENCE** | Something is missing; the drive is to restore it | External / searching | noticing → concern → persistence → relief |
| **V** | **THE MISREADING** | The child believes something that is not true | **Perceptual** | assumption → friction → correction → delight |
| **VI** | **THE CHARGE** | The child becomes responsible for something small | Sustained / caretaking | surprise → duty → tenderness → growth |

Why these six and not others:

- Each has a **different reason the child cannot walk away**, which is what actually distinguishes stories at this age.
- **II is the only family whose obstacle is internal.** This is what makes *courage* a structure rather than a label — and it is precisely what the current corpus lacks, since its "courage" stories give the fear to the companion (§1.4).
- **V is the only family that requires the reader to know something the child does not**, which produces a completely different page rhythm (dramatic irony, gentle and age-appropriate) and a resolution built on *delight* rather than *effort*.
- **VI is the only family whose conflict is duration rather than difficulty** — nothing blocks the child; the demand is care over time. It is the only structure that can land *growth* honestly.
- III and IV feel adjacent but differ on the conflict locus: in III the need is *another's and stated*; in IV the loss is *noticed by the child* and the antagonist is uncertainty, not another's feeling.

Roughly 2–3 blueprints per family, differentiated by **resolution mechanism**, reaches the brief's 10–15 target through structural difference rather than accumulation. Example within Family I: one blueprint resolving by *observation* (the child notices the detail that explains it), one by *deduction* (clues assemble), one by *asking* (understanding requires another's knowledge) — three genuinely different page-6 and page-7 architectures.

### 6.3 Coverage of the MVP brief

The MVP is Adventure / age 5–6 / 8 pages / courage + discovery. Families map to that scope without strain: **I** and **V** carry *discovery*; **II** and **IV** carry *courage*; **III** and **VI** carry the warmth that keeps the library from feeling like eight variations on bravery. All six are viable within adventure; no family requires a world the MVP does not have.

### 6.4 What a blueprint must carry that the current one does not

Minimum for the identified failures to become structurally impossible:

- **Identity** — family, and a one-line *central premise stated semantically* (no nouns).
- **The three mechanisms** (§6.1) as declared fields; they are what the diversity test reads.
- **Emotional arc** — an ordered list of emotional states, one per page, distinct per blueprint. (Currently: no blueprint declares one, and all stories share one by accident.)
- **Per page: role + required story beat + emotional state + state read + state written.** The required beat is the fix for §1.3 and §3 — a page whose beat is *"the child makes a first attempt that does not fully work"* cannot be filled by a sentence about having fun.
- **Semantic content requirements, not IDs** — `companion capable of expressing hesitation`, `discovery that implies someone else was here`, `location capable of concealing something`. This is what makes forest+fox+light and mountain+owl+footprints the same blueprint (§11–12 of the brief).
- **Title families** — plural, structurally different (§20), harvested from V1.
- **Safety ceiling** — one normalised shape: intensity ceiling, forbidden outcomes, required emotional landing.
- **Visual intent per beat** — metadata only, no prompts (§33).
- **A `distinctFrom` note** — what makes this blueprint structurally unlike its siblings. Authored, and checked at review.

### 6.5 Honest combinatorics

The Phase 6 QA report must never quote record counts. The metrics that matter:

- **Narratively distinct arcs** — currently **1**.
- **Unique realised page text** — currently 27 sentences over 156 slots.
- **Expected repeated-sentence count per book** — currently ≈ 3.7 of 8 pages.
- **Unreachable content share** — currently **41%** (66 of 160 records; see §1.6).
- **Choice expressiveness** — how many of the three user choices are observable in the final text. Currently **0 of 3**.

## 7. Turkish language findings

Flagged in Phase 1 because two of these are content-authoring errors that a schema change will not catch, and they will propagate into any new corpus written against the current conventions.

1. **The most-used suffix forms are wrong and fail silently.** Templates write `{{setting.name}}'nde` (8×) and `{{setting.name}}'ne` (5×). Neither `nde` nor `ne` appears in `TurkishSuffixResolver`'s case list, so both fall through to `default: return "'" + cleanedSuffix` — passed through literally, with no vowel harmony and no error. *Fısıldayan Orman* renders as **"Fısıldayan Orman'nde"** (correct: *Ormanda* / *Orman'da*). The two highest-frequency setting constructions in the library produce ungrammatical Turkish on every book, and the unresolved-placeholder check does not catch it because the token *was* substituted.

2. **Apostrophes are applied unconditionally.** The resolver always emits `'` before a suffix. That is correct for proper nouns and wrong for common nouns — and `{{companion.type}}` is a common noun (*tilki*, *baykuş*). V1 contains `{{companion.type}}'ye` → **"tilki'ye"** (correct: *tilkiye*). The resolver needs to know whether a slot is a proper noun; that is a *content* property and belongs in the schema.

3. **`{{child.pronoun}}` / `{{child.possessive}}` are English calques.** `"diye düşündü {{child.pronoun}}"` renders as *"diye düşündü o"* — Turkish drops the subject pronoun; the natural form is *"diye düşündü."* The brief's §21 instruction to remove these is correct, and the evidence supports it: they exist only inside sentences whose structure was conceived in English.

4. **`{{child.favoriteColor}}` (16×) is undeclared at runtime.** It is absent from the generator's variable map, so any V1 variant promoted as-is would leave `{{` in the text and **throw `unresolvedPlaceholders`, failing generation**. A concrete migration hazard, not a cosmetic one.

5. **English leaked into shipped content.** `underwater_friendship_002.json:275` contains `"{{child.name}}'s open hands"` in a `visualScene`.

6. **Repetition discipline is absent.** Nothing prevents the same sentence appearing twice in one book, and §1 shows it happens on ~3.7 pages of 8. For a 5–6-year-old read aloud by a parent, this is the most immediately noticeable quality defect in the product.

## 8. Recommendation for Phase 2

Do not extend the current blueprint schema. It has no narrative fields to extend — designing the layer from the premise in §6.4 is cheaper and will produce a better result than migrating twelve empty records.

Proposed Phase 2 sequence, in order:

1. Ratify the **distinctness rule** (§6.1) and the **six families** (§6.2), or amend them. Everything downstream depends on this being right, so it is the decision worth spending time on.
2. Author `BLUEPRINT_ARCHITECTURE.md` — blueprint schema, state philosophy (minimum viable; the current 15-condition model is both unreachable and larger than needed), compatibility semantics as *semantic traits* rather than IDs, title strategy, and the diversity criteria as a gate.
3. Only then, Phase 3: three pilot blueprints from three *different families* — **II (Threshold)**, **I (Unexplained)**, **III (Appeal)** — matching the brief's recommended diversity but grounded in the taxonomy rather than in labels.

The one open question I would want answered before Phase 2 concludes: **does the "Secret / Discovery" choice select an object, an event, or a semantic discovery-kind?** §4 shows it currently selects an object that never reaches the page. The taxonomy in §6.2 implies it should be a *discovery-kind* — because "footprints" and "a glowing light" and "a strange sound" are the same object-class but recruit different families (footprints imply someone was here → IV or V; a glowing light implies something unexplained → I). If that choice is modelled as an object, blueprint selection loses its most informative input. I recommend modelling it as a semantic discovery-kind that *binds* an object and/or event internally, and will proceed on that assumption in Phase 2 unless directed otherwise.
