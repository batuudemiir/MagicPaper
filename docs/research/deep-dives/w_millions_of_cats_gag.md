## Summary (paraphrase)

A very old couple are lonely and the wife wishes for a cat. The husband walks over the hills to a hill covered with countless cats, cannot choose because each one seems as pretty as the last, and leads them all home; on the way they drink a pond dry and graze the hills bare. The wife says they cannot feed them all and suggests the cats decide which one to keep. Every cat claims to be the prettiest and they fight until none can be seen; the couple fear they have eaten each other. One thin, frightened kitten survives because it thought itself plain and said nothing. They wash and feed it until it is plump, and both declare it the most beautiful cat in the world.

## Strengths

- one refrain carried by five different speakers, the last one turning it into a declaration of love for one cat
- absurd scale (a pond in one sip each) told in plain sentences and shown in the pictures
- a clear folktale rhythm: wish, journey, excess, consequence, a small survivor, a cosy home
- the theme of modesty and of love making something beautiful is shown through events, not stated
- a quiet wordless last picture that resolves the opening loneliness

## Weaknesses

- the problem is resolved by the implied mutual destruction of the crowd, which the couple treat lightly
- the protagonist causes the problem and does nothing to solve it; the wife's plan and the cats' quarrel do
- the kitten's worth is first measured by looks; it is called pretty after it grows plump
- long sentences, some over 30 words when read from the page images, and dense adult cadence ('the very old man') for 5-6 listeners
