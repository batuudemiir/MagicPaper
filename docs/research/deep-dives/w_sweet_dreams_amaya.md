## Summary (paraphrase)

A boy dreams of a parade of Indian sweets. Each sweet gets a short rhyming verse that hands over to the next one, often through a question. After the warmest sweet he wakes with a start on a rooftop cot at night, calls the sweets back, shuts his eyes and returns to the dream to dance with them.

## Repetition

- structure: each sweet gets a verse opening with its name said twice, then hands over to the next sweet — functions rhythm, anticipation, participation; effective: True — a predictable chant that a listener can join; the hand-over makes each page turn expected
- cumulative: the waking page lists all the sweets again — functions memory, comfort; effective: True — gathers the whole dream back before sleep
- callback: the last page returns to the dream world of the first — functions comfort; effective: True — closes the circle: dream, wake, dream

## Visual storytelling

- Only the waking picture shows the real setting: a rope cot on a rooftop at night, and that the dream cat is his own cat.
- The two night pages (12-13) are the only dark pages; colour carries the dream/waking change.
- On p9 Faizu already floats with closed eyes: the picture prepares the waking.

## Strengths

- A chant structure that hands each page to the next (question or named next sweet): strong anticipation for a read-aloud.
- The real-world turn comes late (p12 of 14) and is quickly soothed; the book ends inside sleep.
- A rhyme that completes across the page turn (p11 to p12) makes the waking the strongest turn.

## Weaknesses

- Almost no story problem until p12; the middle is a list.
- Food words (Indian sweets) need context for readers outside India.
