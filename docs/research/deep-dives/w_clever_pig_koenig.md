## Summary (paraphrase)

A child plays find-the-carrot with Pig, who finds it under the broom. The next morning Pig's sty is empty; the child is comforted, searches all day and says how much they miss their friend. Pig returns with three piglets, the game starts again with all of them, and the day ends with goodnight at the sty.

## Repetition

- refrain: The praise for Pig returns at the found carrot and at the reunion — functions memory, comfort; effective: True — the reunion echoes the first game, so the listener feels the friendship restored
- phrase: The invitation to play opens the book, is repeated at the empty sty and returns for all the pigs — functions comfort, memory, rhythm; effective: True — the same invitation marks ordinary day, loss and return; its third use includes the new piglets
- predictable_pattern: Two parallel negatives during the search; a one-two-three count of the new friends — functions participation, rhythm; effective: True — very short, sayable patterns for the youngest listeners

## Strengths

- an extremely short text (68 words, longest sentence 6) that still carries loss and return
- dramatic irony carried entirely by one picture
- a circular ending at the sty, with the first game replayed by more players
- a sad middle that is named in a plain sentence and comforted in the picture
- a calm, dark last spread for a goodnight

## Weaknesses

- the child does not find Pig; Pig simply comes back
- the reason for the absence depends on reading the pictures; read aloud without them, the piglets appear unexplained
- the child and the adult are unnamed

## Interpretation (book-research, 28 Oct 2026; one book, not a finding)

- OBSERVED: the peak is a plateau (pp. 7-9, each 4/5; engine scale 3) across loss, reunion and discovery. It starts at position 0.55 and reaches the last third only at p9.
- OBSERVED: on p7 the picture tells the reader the secret. Pig and her piglets stand hidden in the bushes while the line says the child misses a friend. Why Pig left is never said in the text.
- OBSERVED: sadness is named in one plain sentence (p7) and comforted only in the picture (p5: the child on an adult's lap). The adult does not resolve anything, because Pig comes back on her own.
- OBSERVED: a bedtime-framed close. The invitation to play returns (pp. 1, 4, 10), tub, broom and sty come back, and the sty that was empty on p4 is full on the last spread. The last spread is the only dark one, with two words.
- INFERRED: without pictures the piglets arrive unexplained, so a text-only version would have to state the reason in words (m005).
