## Summary (paraphrase)

A boy wants someone to play the stone game diketo with him; his mother, father and brother are each busy, so he sits alone and then starts playing by himself, and his fun draws the whole family out to join him. A small blue frog, never mentioned in the words, follows and mirrors him on every spread.

## Repetition

- structure: Ask-and-refusal pattern: Jabu asks one family member to come and play, and the next line begins with 'But' and names what keeps them busy — functions participation, anticipation, rhythm; effective: True — three identical asks let a listener say the request with the reader, and the third refusal sets up the low point
- callback: Each refusal is undone in reverse: Dad stops his digging, Mama brings out the food she was cooking, the brother leaves his reading — functions memory, comfort; effective: True — the ending answers each refusal by name, so the resolution feels earned rather than announced
- callback: The big hole Dad digs and the small hole Jabu digs for diketo — functions memory; effective: True — a concrete echo that links the father's work to the child's play

## Visual storytelling

- OBSERVED: the turning point that Dad has joined the game is shown only in the picture (he kneels at the hole with stones) on spread 9; the words only say Mama brings food.

## Strengths

- a clear wish stated on the first spread
- three-step refusal pattern a listener can join
- the child's own action, not an adult's decision, turns the story
- the resolution undoes each refusal by name
- a silent companion carries emotion for pre-readers
- a wordless payoff spread

## Weaknesses

- the solo-play turn passes quickly (one spread) for a book about being alone
- family members join without dialogue, so the reunion is told mostly through pictures
