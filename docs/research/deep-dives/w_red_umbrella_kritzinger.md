## Summary (paraphrase)

A wordless book: Dad brings home a new red umbrella and hangs it up, out of bounds, to keep for a rainy day. One by one the children, Grandma and Mom secretly use it for everything but rain: a den, a paper-plane target, a carrot basket, a sunshade, a walking stick. When the storm finally comes and Dad needs it, the hook is empty; the family finds it upside down in the yard, full of rain, a pool for the household duck.

## Repetition

- action: the same forbidden object reappears in a new secret use on each spread — functions anticipation, participation, humor; effective: True — a wordless pattern the child can predict and name ('now Grandma has it!')
- callback: the empty wall hook (p2 full, p7 and p10 empty) marks the rule and its failure — functions memory; effective: True — one recurring prop carries the story's question without words

## Visual storytelling

- Wordless: all information is in the picture, so every page-turn effect is visual.
- Page 9 is a four-panel time-lapse of the same skyline: a wordless countdown to the rain.
- Pages 11-12 split a reveal across the turn: faces staring at the window, then what they saw.
- The duck appears on every spread as a find-it companion.

## Strengths

- one object, many uses: a simple pattern a pre-reader can follow alone
- the rule is broken by adults too, so no one is singled out
- the climax reveal is held across a page turn

## Weaknesses

- wordless: the 'saved for a rainy day' premise is only on the publisher's blurb; a young reader may not infer Dad's intent
- pages 3-8 are a list of uses with low stakes until the rain arrives

## Anti-patterns observed

- unclear_stakes (pages [3, 4, 5, 6]): nothing is at risk while the umbrella is borrowed; tension only comes with the rain
