## Summary (paraphrase)

A nine-year-old who is always told she is too small dreams of a quest for a bravery drink; she crosses a river, finds her way and passes a frightening cave on her own ideas, wakes before drinking, and her grandmother shows her she was brave already.

## Repetition

- structure: three near-quits ('I think I will give up because…') each followed by her own idea — functions anticipation, participation; effective: True — each obstacle escalates (river, lost, frightening cave) and each is solved differently, so the pattern builds rather than repeats
- structure: step counting the reader joins (50, 100, 200, total) — functions participation, rhythm; effective: None — invites participation and carries the count across a page turn, but the arithmetic asides also interrupt the story's tension
- callback: Gogo's 'I believe in you' at bedtime answered by 'you ARE brave' in the morning; the not-as-wise/strong/brave list returns — functions memory, comfort; effective: True — the ending answers the exact doubt the beginning set up

## Visual storytelling

- The bedroom is drawn twice, night with stars (p4) and sunrise (p14): the picture frames the dream.
- The giraffe appears in the picture on p9 before the text needs it, planting the next solution.
- The frightening eyes (p11) are drawn as glowing dots in the dark; the next page redraws them as friendly fireflies.

## Strengths

- each obstacle is solved by the child's own observation (leaf to raft, giraffe to tree, looking again at the eyes)
- bedtime frame: blessing at night, reframing in the morning; the adventure lives inside a dream
- the fear peak is defused on the very next page

## Weaknesses

- maths and comprehension asides (ages, branches, total steps) interrupt the story's tension
- the narrator addresses the reader often, which flattens immersion
- long for a picture book (15 story pages, longer paragraphs)

## Anti-patterns observed

- moral_stated_not_shown (pages [15]): mild: the lesson is enacted across pages 7-13, then also spoken by Gogo
- unnecessary_exposition (pages [2, 8]): arithmetic questions add explanation the story does not need
