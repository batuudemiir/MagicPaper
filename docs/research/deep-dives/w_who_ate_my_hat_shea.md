## Summary (paraphrase)

A meerkat who adores its yellow hat finds it gone and asks four animals in turn whether they ate it; each says no and names its own food, until the picture shows the zebra eating flowers off the very hat. The zebra apologises, the two make matching new hats, and every suspect ends up at one sunset picnic.

## Strengths

- a repeated question the listener can join after one round
- the picture lets the reader solve the mystery before the hero
- apology and making something together as the repair, not punishment
- circular opening and closing line

## Weaknesses

- as text alone the reveal is lost: the zebra's line reads as another innocent denial
- no animal is named, so a listener without pictures cannot tell the suspects apart
- the zebra's innocence (not knowing it was a hat) is inferred from a face only

## Interpretation (analyst, one book)

This is one book; everything below is a hypothesis.

- INFERRED: the mystery is solved by the reader one spread before the hero (page 6), so the page turn to page 7 pays off something the listener already suspects. In a text-only telling this reveal disappears: the zebra's line reads as one more denial.
- OBSERVED: the peak (page 7 of 12, research 4/5, about 2 on the engine's 0-3 scale) sits in the middle third, followed by a wordless pause and a wordless repair. The resolution comes from a peer's apology and shared making, with no adult present.
- OBSERVED: the opening line returns at the end, spoken by both characters: a solo line turns into a shared one.
