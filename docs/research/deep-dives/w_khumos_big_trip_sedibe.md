## Summary (paraphrase)

A girl on a long car trip with her parents keeps asking whether they have arrived; they point out goats and ostriches until they reach a town with an enormous hole, where her father explains that miners once dug there for diamonds; on the way home she wonders whether there are diamonds under her own house.

## Repetition

- phrase: the child's arrival question and the parents' 'not yet' reply recur across the trip, ending on 'almost' and then 'we are here' — functions participation, anticipation, humor; effective: True — a question every listener knows from car trips; the 'almost' variant counts down to arrival
- structure: single-word lines piled up in threes: counting ostriches; here, there, everywhere; three pick sounds — functions rhythm, participation; effective: True — short joinable words and a count to follow on the picture
- callback: diamonds in the ground return as diamonds under her own house — functions memory; effective: True — carries the place home into the child's own world

## Visual storytelling

- The car windows frame the landscape like pages; the view changes while the question stays the same.
- The destination is named only on a road sign in the picture.
- The history of the mine (men digging under a watching overseer) is only in the picture; the text gives only the sound of picks.
- Two-colour line drawing; the hole is the only large solid dark shape, which gives it weight.

## Interpretation (analyst, one book)

- OBSERVED: the car-trip question recurs on four spreads (pp. 2, 4, 5, 6) with a parent's 'not yet' each time; the fifth reply becomes 'almost' (p. 6) and the sixth 'we are here' (p. 7). The pattern ends where the story turns, which works as a countdown.
- OBSERVED: the book hands the reader a task. On p. 10 the reader is asked to find a diamond, and diamonds are drawn on the spread to find. This is a direct question answered by the picture, not by the next spread.
- OBSERVED: what only the picture says matters here. The destination is named only on a road sign (p. 7), and the history of the mine (men digging under a man in a hat who watches them, p. 11) is given in words only as the sound of picks. A text-only telling would lose both.
- INFERRED: the ending carries the trip home. The last spread is a quiet drive home at sunset that ends on the child's own question about her house, an open, calm ending (research intensity 2 of 5, about 1 on the engine's 0-3 scale) with no hook that needs answering.
- The teddy bear is on almost every spread, but its expression stays fixed. It goes along with Khumo but does not mirror her feelings.
