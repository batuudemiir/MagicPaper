## Summary (paraphrase)

Livhu is a cheerful girl who uses a wheelchair. Adults stare and pity her, and a shop cashier pushes her to the front of the queue when she wants to stay and chat. Children in the park ask first before pushing her down a slope, and she loves it. She knows people mean well, but mostly she likes to do things herself: she chats in the shop queue, makes friends, pulls in a tug-of-war, and falls asleep thinking of more adventures with her new friends.

## Strengths

- a sentence broken on BUT across the page turn carries the book's idea
- consent before help is shown by contrast, not lectured
- the protagonist voices her own wish and shows it in action
- the same two places return to show the change
- a calm bedtime close after a high moment

## Weaknesses

- the tug-of-war peak is dropped: the rope is drawn tearing and the next page never says what happened
- the message is partly stated (her wheelchair helps her have fun)
- sentences reach 27 words with long speaker tags
- episodic: the change from pity to friendship is a sequence of scenes, not one problem worked through

## Anti-patterns observed

- moral_stated_not_shown (pages [6, 7, 11]): the idea is stated by the narrator (she knows people want to help, but ...; her wheelchair helps her have fun); softened because pages 3 and 8 also show it
