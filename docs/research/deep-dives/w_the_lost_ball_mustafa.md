## Summary (paraphrase)

A boy runs indoors to fetch his ball for a waiting game and finds his room too messy to search; place by place he looks and fails while, in the pictures, each place he searches ends up tidy; his friends give up and leave, and only when the room is clean does he spot the box where the ball was, and they play the next day.

## Repetition

- structure: question to the reader 'Is the ball on/near …?' then the answer page 'No, it's not!' — functions participation, anticipation, rhythm; effective: True — the listener answers before the turn, and each answer page quietly shows a tidied place
- refrain: 'No, it's not!' returns, the third time extended — functions participation, humor; effective: True — a sayable answer the child can shout
- callback: 'Hold on!' from the opening returns when Jami calls back; the room picture from p4 returns tidy on p15 — functions memory; effective: True — lets the reader see the change the text only states late

## Visual storytelling

- The tidying is shown only in the pictures (pp6, 8, 12) until the text names it on p15.
- The ball shows at the top of the box on p16 before it is found.
- A cat companion, never mentioned in the text, mirrors Jami's dismay and worry (pp3, 10).
- One wordless page (p14) carries his anger.

## Strengths

- a question-answer pattern the listener can join on every turn
- a hidden second story (tidying) told by the pictures and named at the turn
- the child finds the ball himself; no adult
- a cost that matters (friends leave) before the solution

## Weaknesses

- 18 pages is long for the pattern; the third search adds little new
- the lesson about tidying is implied, which is good, but the ending skips the friends' reaction
