## Summary (paraphrase)

A village goat chasing the metal rooster on a church gets stuck on its roof; one neighbour after another offers a better idea to bring him down, and he eats each one until the carpenter's ramp lets him slide to the girl who owns him, and the whole crowd tells her not to let him out again.

## Repetition

- structure: 'but Rocky wanted to eat the …' followed by a neighbour offering a better idea — functions anticipation, humor, participation; effective: True — the listener can predict that each new idea will be eaten, and the pattern breaks when the ramp works
- phrase: 'I have an idea' / 'I have a better idea' spoken by three helpers in turn — functions rhythm, participation; effective: True — a short sayable line that marks each new attempt
- callback: the opening complaint about who let the goat out returns as the cast's closing chorus; p10 lists every helper — functions memory, humor; effective: True — closes the circle and recaps the cast for the reader

## Visual storytelling

- Two of the three rescue ideas (the box tower, the plank ramp) are shown only in the pictures; the text names who has an idea but not what it is.
- The weathercock the goat was chasing is in the picture from p2, before the text names it.
- The climax page carries one shouted line; the picture shows the ramp being raised.

## Strengths

- a clear try-fail-try pattern with a sayable line on each attempt
- the final question to the reader is answered on the last page by the whole cast
- comic animal behaviour carries the tension without real danger

## Weaknesses

- six named adults in eleven pages; only the recap on p10 helps the reader keep them apart
- the child owner only begs and shouts; adults solve the problem
- as text alone the rescue ideas are not explained (they live in the pictures)

## Anti-patterns observed

- too_many_characters (pages [1, 5, 6, 7, 10]): four named neighbours plus parents and the child for a 5-6 listener; the cumulative recap softens it
- adult_rescues_child (pages [7, 8, 9]): the carpenter's ramp brings the child's goat down; Rose has no idea of her own
