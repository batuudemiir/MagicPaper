## Summary (paraphrase)

In class, Dumisani dares Dudu to throw her school hat through a high window. Their first throws miss and one lands on a stuffed rhino, which sets them laughing; Dumisani then gets his through while Dudu keeps missing until she gives up in a huff. Dumisani shows her how to aim and throw, her hat sails out, they cheer, and only then realise both hats are gone and shout for the teacher.

## Repetition

- predictable_pattern: A one-two-three count before every throw, first by Dudu, then twice by Dumisani — functions participation, anticipation, rhythm; effective: True — a listener can count along and knows a throw is coming; the count frames each attempt as a round of a game
- phrase: The call to go again, from Dudu after the first laugh and from Dumisani after her misses — functions rhythm, humor; effective: True — the same word changes meaning: delight from Dudu, pressure from Dumisani, which sets up her quitting
- structure: Throw, describe where each hat lands, react: three rounds plus a coached fourth — functions anticipation, memory; effective: True — rule of three failures then success with a twist; variation in where the hats land keeps it from being repetitive

## Strengths

- a clear rule-of-three attempt structure with a twist ending
- a peer (not an adult) teaches the skill, and the friend changes from tease to helper
- a wordless realisation page placed right after the biggest cheer
- a picture-only comic witness (the rhino) gives the reader something extra to spot
- feelings shown in faces rather than stated

## Weaknesses

- the rule-breaking (throwing things out of the window) is never questioned in the text

## Interpretation (book-research, 28 Oct 2026; one book, not a finding)

- OBSERVED: the biggest cheer (p10, 5/5; engine scale 3) is followed by a wordless spread (p11) where both children realise the hats are gone. The page turn, not the text, flips victory into the problem.
- OBSERVED: the skill that solves the problem comes from a peer, not an adult. Dumisani coaches the throw on p9, and the step-by-step vignettes show how it works. The teacher is only named in the last line and never drawn. This is a counterexample to an adult making the resolution possible.
- OBSERVED: a stuffed rhino, planted on p1, reacts on five spreads (wears a hat, cheers, is shocked, smiles), and the text never mentions it. Its reactions mirror the children's, so it is a picture-only companion.
- INFERRED: read aloud without pictures, Dudu's feelings (disappointment, frustration, embarrassment) disappear, because the text gives only actions. Under rule m005, a text-only MagicPaper version would have to name them.
- OBSERVED: the sentences are long for 5-6 (mean 8.1, longest 24) and fail a ≤14-word ceiling. The book is set in large dialogue type with short narrative lines.
