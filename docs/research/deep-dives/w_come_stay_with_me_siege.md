## Summary (paraphrase)

When a young turtle's grandmother is hurt and his parents leave to help her, his bunny friend invites him to stay in her tree; they play, eat and sleep side by side, and when the family returns Granny gives him her flute and everyone dances.

## Repetition

- structure: 'They <verb> all day' / 'They <verb> all night' pair two short sentences across the stay — functions rhythm, comfort; effective: True — the parallel compresses a day and a night into a pattern a pre-reader can hear, and makes the time apart feel short and safe
- callback: butterflies recur on the play, return and gift spreads — functions memory, comfort; effective: True — a picture motif marks happy moments without words

## Visual storytelling

- Granny's injury is explained only in a thought bubble (page 2).
- Page 6 is wordless and carries the funniest moments.
- The flute and the bandage are planted in the picture on page 8 before the text names them.
- In the download, after the last text spread come a decorative butterfly-and-squirrel pattern and a small square vignette repeating the night scene; neither is part of the story pages analysed here.

## Strengths

- a separation story told as an adventure in a friend's home
- the stay compressed into a calm day-night parallel
- celebration that joins both worlds at the end
- clues planted in pictures before the text

## Weaknesses

- Tendai's feelings during the stay are not shown in words; the separation worry is only implied
- the flute arrives without earlier set-up in the text
