## Summary (paraphrase)

A wordless book. Neo gets a goldfish for her birthday and it turns out to be a genius. When she goes out to play ball with Dad, the fish, left behind, works out a plan and floats away in its bowl under party balloons, over the city to the sea. Birds pop the balloons and the fish drops into the sea, makes friends, is chased by an octopus and hides in a giant clam that shuts on it. Neo, in a diving suit with the cat, prises the clam open; they hug and swim home, where Dad waves from an underwater house.

## Visual storytelling

- Wordless: all information is in the pictures, including the fish's motive (left out on page 3) and how the plan works (thought cloud on page 4).
- Planted objects pay off: balloons on page 1 lift the bowl on page 4; the octopus peeks from rocks before filling the page (8); the sparkling clam at the page edge (9) becomes the trap (10).
- The darkest moment is a colour change: page 10 left is dull grey and brown, the only drained page in the book.
- Quiet wide spread (5) between two busy ones gives a breath before the fall.
- The cat reacts on most spreads where the family appears (question mark, fur on end, hug).

## Strengths

- planted objects make each turn fair (balloons, octopus, clam)
- a child rescues the protagonist; the adult only waits at home
- the low point and the rescue share one spread, so peril never sits alone over a turn
- a full arc (home, away, danger, rescue, home) told without words

## Weaknesses

- the protagonist's cleverness does not solve the climax; it causes the trouble and is not used again
- how Neo knew where to dive is not shown
- the octopus threat is not resolved, only escaped
- wordless: the fish's motive (loneliness or following Neo) is open to reading
