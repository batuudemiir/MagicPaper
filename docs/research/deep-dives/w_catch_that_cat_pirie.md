## Summary (paraphrase)

A boy and his dog chase a cat that stole his sock through a tree, a farm and a pond; when he finally catches her he finds the sock was a bed for her kittens and takes them all home.

## Repetition

- refrain: 'Catch that cat!' closes most spreads; the last spread turns it into 'Catch all those cats!' — functions participation, rhythm, anticipation; effective: True — the callback changes meaning at the end
- phrase: doubled imperatives: 'Hurry. Hurry!', 'Look. Look!', 'Quick. Fast.' — functions rhythm; effective: True — keeps read-aloud pace

## Visual storytelling

- First-person text, so the boy never describes himself; the pictures carry him and the dog.
- A sideways spread makes the reader rotate the book at the moment the cat floats.
- The reveal (kittens) is a picture first; the text names it after.

## Strengths

- a protagonist who stops and makes a plan
- a final reveal that reframes the antagonist
- very high read-aloud rhythm
