## Summary (paraphrase)

An unseen voice keeps calling a ginger cat back as it hides, climbs, slips outdoors, unravels a sock, finds a snail and runs into a hen; it comes home and is told to nap in its basket, but in the end it is found asleep on the bed instead.

## Repetition

- refrain: the call for the cat to come back opens six spreads, each followed by a new short reason — functions participation, anticipation, rhythm, humor; effective: True — a listener can join the call, and each turn reveals where the cat has gone
- callback: the basket instruction of page 2 returns on page 10 as nap time — functions memory, comfort; effective: True — frames the outing as away and home again
- structure: the call to come back is inverted into a greeting when the cat returns — functions comfort, humor; effective: True — the change in the familiar line marks the pattern's end
- cumulative: thread, the snail and feathers collect on the cat from page 6 to the last spread, only in the pictures — functions memory, humor; effective: True — the cat's body becomes a record of its outing

## Visual storytelling

- The text is almost only instructions from an unseen voice; what the cat actually does is carried by the pictures on every spread.
- Two of twelve spreads are wordless (5 and 12); the ending twist exists only in the picture.
- Objects the text points at without naming (what is not yours, what you have got, who is not your friend) are shown only in the pictures.
- The cat carries a growing collection of thread, a snail and feathers: a visual memory of the outing.

## Strengths

- a joinable refrain with a new picture answer each time
- the pattern breaks twice: the greeting when the cat returns, then the empty basket
- a wordless visual punchline ending that rewards looking

## Weaknesses

- read aloud without pictures the story is mostly lost: 5 of 12 spreads depend on the picture to be understood
- the unseen voice is not a character a child can see or identify with
