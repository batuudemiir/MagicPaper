## Repetition

- structure: Short 'Fifi does X with Teddy' sentences list the shared routines — functions rhythm, participation; effective: True — a pre-reader can predict and join the pattern, and the pattern establishes what is lost
- callback: Teddy drawn on a blue disc (page 1) returns as a thought bubble on the wordless search spreads and as the blue speech shape — functions memory, anticipation; effective: True — a visual refrain carries the longing without words
- callback: The final hug repeats the cover image — functions comfort; effective: True — closes the book on the opening picture

## Visual storytelling

- Two consecutive wordless spreads (6-7) carry the whole search; the text skips from 'not there' to Mom's question.
- The clue (a basin of soapy water beside Mom, page 8) is shown before the text resolves it — dramatic irony for the child who looks.
- The answer on page 10 is in the picture (Teddy on the washing line) at the same moment as the text.
- The ending spread is wordless and echoes the cover.

## Strengths

- a very small, real stake (a toy in the wash) told at full emotional weight
- wordless search spreads let the reader do the searching
- clue planted in the picture before the reveal
- short sentences a 3-4 year old can follow

## Weaknesses

- the adult solves it; Fifi's search does not lead to the answer
- the setup takes four of twelve pages before anything happens

## Anti-patterns observed

- adult_rescues_child (pages [10]): Mom explains where Teddy is; mild, because the stake is a misunderstanding, not danger.
