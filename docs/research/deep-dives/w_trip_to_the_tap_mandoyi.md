## Summary (paraphrase)

A girl goes with her mother at dawn to fetch water at the communal tap, playing with two friends while the adults fill the buckets. In the afternoon her grandmother needs water for supper, but the girl's pink bucket has vanished; after a fruitless search the grandmother turns a plastic jug into a new painted bucket. The next day the two friends bring jugs of their own, the grandmother paints them in their favourite colours, and the three children are eager to head to the tap with their new buckets.

## Strengths

- a small, concrete, relatable loss (a child's own bucket) with a visible emotional low and recovery
- reuse is shown, not preached: a jug becomes a bucket and the idea spreads to friends
- the lost bucket's margin thread gives a pre-reader something to find on every spread
- short, plain sentences; mean under 8 words, longest 14
- a warm three-generation household and a communal setting

## Weaknesses

- the child's only action toward the problem is a failed search; the grandmother solves it
- the story ends on the question of going to the tap; the trip with the new buckets is never shown, so the second half loses tension
- the second movement (friends' buckets) repeats the solution without a new difficulty
- the lost bucket is never resolved in the text, which some readers may find unfinished

## Anti-patterns observed

- adult_rescues_child (pages [7, 8]): the grandmother has the plan and makes the bucket; the child receives it
- passive_protagonist (pages [7, 8, 11]): mild: Oluhle watches and thanks; she does not take part in the making in text or picture
- weak_ending (pages [12]): mild: ends on an unanswered question before the promised trip; the closing vignette of the lost bucket partly compensates
