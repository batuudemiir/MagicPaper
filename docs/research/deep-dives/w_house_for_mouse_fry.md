## Summary (paraphrase)

Mouse sets out at night to find a new home and slips into a human house. Three animals in turn, a puppy, a parrot and a fish, kindly offer their own beds, and Mouse thanks each one; each night the dream takes on the host's world and goes badly (bouncy, wild, cold). Mouse then decides he needs warmth and dryness, runs off alone, finds the family's fire and a bookshelf beside it, and sleeps well at last. A small closing picture says good night.

## Repetition

- refrain: the offer line and Mouse's thanks repeat with only the host's name changed — functions participation, anticipation, rhythm; effective: True — a two-line frame a pre-reader can say along; the changing host is the only new information
- structure: 'That night' dream sentence with two adjectives after each offer, turned positive at the end — functions anticipation, comfort, memory; effective: True — the pattern sets up the final variation (warm and cosy), so the resolution is felt as the pattern coming right
- callback: the closing vignette repeats the cover and opening image of Mouse on the patterned hill — functions comfort, memory; effective: True — a visual circle for a bedtime close

## Visual storytelling

- Text always sits alone on the white left page; the picture is on the right, so text and picture are cleanly separated on every spread.
- Each dream picture takes the texture of the host's home (basket, jungle, under water): the reason each night fails is in the picture.
- Page 10 is the only wordless full spread: the fire and the family are shown before the text names the solution.
- On page 11 Mouse is absent from the picture; he has hidden on the shelf.
- The last page is a tiny vignette on white, the quietest page in the book.

## Strengths

- a three-offer pattern with a fourth, positive variation: the clearest repeated frame in a short book
- the protagonist solves the problem himself after three kind but unsuitable offers
- pictures carry the reason for each failure without any blame on the hosts
- a quiet, small last page fits a bedtime read

## Weaknesses

- Mouse is passive for six spreads, simply accepting
- the hosts never react to Mouse leaving; their kindness is not acknowledged at the end
- the human family is a backdrop only
- the bookshelf spread does not show Mouse, which a young reader may find confusing without help

## Anti-patterns observed

- passive_protagonist (pages [3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8]): Mouse only accepts and suffers until page 9; acceptable for 3-4 because the pattern is the pleasure
