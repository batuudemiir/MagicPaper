## Summary (paraphrase)

A small boy is startled four times (by something in a bush, a crash at bedtime, a box by the door, a dark empty house) and each time cries out for Mmawe; each time a voice tells him not to be scared and names the harmless cause: a new dog, a falling pot, new shoes, and finally his father coming home.

## Repetition

- refrain: The title cry, the boy screaming for Mmawe, on four spreads — functions participation, anticipation, humor; effective: True — a listener can shout it with him; the pose and lettering never change, only the background, which darkens at the end
- refrain: The answer line 'don't be scared, Tshedza, it's just ...' on four spreads, each with a new ordinary cause — functions comfort, anticipation, participation; effective: True — the predictable comfort line lets the child expect safety; only the cause changes, so the child guesses it
- structure: Unknown, scream, harmless answer: four rounds — functions rhythm, comfort; effective: True — short rounds that always land safely

## Visual storytelling

- The picture lets the listener see the answer before the boy does: a tail in the bush on page 1, a snake-like red lace curling out of the box on page 7.
- The comforting adult is cropped to the waist on pages 3 and 9, keeping the camera at the child's height; her face appears only in the bedtime kiss.
- The fourth scream reuses the same drawing with every colour dimmed: escalation carried by palette alone.
- White space and hand lettering give each scream a full page of its own, a quiet frame for a loud line.

## Strengths

- a refrain a pre-reader can shout
- a second refrain that always delivers comfort
- the picture lets the child solve the scare first
- ordinary causes make fear safe to play with

## Weaknesses

- the child never resolves a fear himself; an adult always explains
- no growth: he screams the same way at the end

## Interpretation (analyst, one book)

One book is one book; everything below is a hypothesis.

- OBSERVED: Two refrains work as a pair: the child's cry (four times) and the adult's comfort line (four times). The second always follows the first after a page turn, so the listener can expect safety each time the fear repeats.
- OBSERVED: Twice the picture lets the listener solve the scare first (a tail in the bush, a lace that looks like a snake curling from the box).
- INFERRED: Escalation is carried by the palette. The fourth scream reuses the same drawing with every colour dimmed, and the dark-house spread before it is the book's peak (4 of 5; engine scale about 3 of 3), answered at once on the last spread.
- OBSERVED: The resolution is always an adult naming the cause, and the last answer is a parent coming home. For 3-4 this reads as comfort; as a model for older bands it is a passive protagonist.
- OBSERVED: A bedtime episode sits in the middle (pages 4-6: a crash, a scream, a tuck-in and a good-night kiss), but the book is not framed by sleep and ends at its loudest emotional point, not a quiet one.
