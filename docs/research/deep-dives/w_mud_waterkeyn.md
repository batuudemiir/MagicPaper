## Summary (paraphrase)

A girl's mud friend comes alive and makes a mess across the yard and kitchen; each time her mother arrives the girl is blamed, and when the friend finally jumps into her bath she decides it can stay there.

## Repetition

- refrain: 'It's not me, Mama. It's Mud!' answers each accusation — functions participation, anticipation, humor; effective: True — the reader knows the line and the truth; the third use changes to a plea, so it escalates instead of repeating
- structure: mischief → Mama's question → blame, three times — functions rhythm, anticipation; effective: True — each cycle enlarges the mess

## Visual storytelling

- The picture carries the central irony: Mud vanishes whenever Mama appears, so the reader sides with the child.
- Sound words (SQUELCH!, WHEEEE!, KER-SPLASH!) get their own large type and a half-spread splat.
- Page 10 answers 'where is Mud?' with Mud's feet under the sheet before the next spread reveals it.

## Strengths

- text–picture irony that rewards the child reader
- a refrain the listener can join
- a calm spread (bath) placed just before the loudest one

## Weaknesses

- the ending is a joke rather than a resolution; the blame is never cleared
