## Summary (paraphrase)

Banzi and Gogo, the grandmother, love her garden and its bees; after a few days away they find the bees and flowers gone. Gogo promises a magic trick: plant new sunflowers, leave out water and call the bees with a chant. For a week nothing seems to happen, while the pictures show the seeds rooting and sprouting under the soil; then the flowers bloom and the bees return.

## Repetition

- refrain: A buzzing sound call-and-answer between Banzi, Gogo and the bees — functions participation, rhythm; effective: True — a sound word small listeners can say along; it also establishes what is lost when the bees go
- refrain: Gogo's calling chant, repeated twice inside itself, then echoed by Banzi in a whisper with one word changed — functions participation, memory; effective: True — the child echoes the adult, modelling how a listener can join
- phrase: The same two-word report that nothing has happened yet, on two consecutive spreads — functions anticipation, humor; effective: True — the words stay still while the pictures move (roots, then shoots), so the turn becomes a game of seeing more than the characters

## Strengths

- a sound refrain to say along
- the picture runs ahead of the text during the wait, rewarding close looking
- a real-world cause (flowers and water bring bees) framed as play
- calm, unhurried waiting shown across several spreads
- warm grandparent-child pairing

## Weaknesses

- the child does what the adult says; the plan and the chant both come from Gogo
- framing a natural process as 'magic' leaves the cause unstated for a child who does not read the pictures closely
- faceless collage figures make feeling depend on gesture alone

## Anti-patterns observed

- passive_protagonist (pages [5, 6, 7, 8]): Banzi follows Gogo's plan and echoes her chant; the child does not originate the solution (INFERRED)
