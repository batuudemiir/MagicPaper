## Summary (paraphrase)

Two king penguin chicks who do everything together walk far from their colony, laugh at an odd-looking penguin with head plumes and leave him alone; after they moult into young adults and need someone to teach them to swim, that same penguin offers, and the three swim together.

## Visual storytelling

- 5 of 12 spreads are wordless and they carry the moral line: the stranger left alone (p6) and the friendship (p11-12) exist only in pictures.
- Read without pictures the text is a plain growing-up story; the exclusion-and-repair arc disappears.
- Scale tells distance: the chicks shrink to dots on a wide ice field (p3).
- The moult is drawn as a full spread of drifting feathers with no character (p8): time passing as texture.
- Watercolour, lots of white page; text sits on white, never over the art.
- Spoken lines carry no quotation marks, so the CLI dialogue_ratio of 0 understates speech: p5, p7, p9 and p10 are spoken.

## Strengths

- the opening lack (too young to swim) becomes the climax's need
- the outsider they mocked is the one who helps; the reversal is shown, not preached
- very short lines suited to read-aloud by two voices
- wordless spreads give the listener room to tell what happened

## Weaknesses

- the pair never acknowledge or repair the teasing in words; the reconciliation is implied by pictures only
- the name 'oakum boys' is explained only in the back-matter note
- spoken lines carry no quotation marks or speaker tags, so who speaks on p10 depends on the picture

- resolves_too_quickly (pages [9, 10]): the teasing is never revisited; the stranger simply volunteers
