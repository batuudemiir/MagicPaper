## Summary (paraphrase)

Alex feels unwell and his mother takes him to Dr Bongi, who gives him medicine. He finds medicine yucky and tries to avoid it: he offers Jett his toy car to drink it, pours it on a sandwich, buries it in the garden and hides it in a bird's nest. Each plan fails. At last he wonders whether to just try it; the medicine turns into an imagined green sea he rides, and he feels much better while his family says they are proud.

## Repetition

- structure: an idea spread (thinking face with question marks, then a lightbulb page with 'I know, I'll ...') followed by a failure spread (one interjection on a lime page, the result on white) — functions anticipation, humor, participation; effective: True — after the first round the listener predicts the failure and can say the cry; the layout itself (yellow bulb, lime cry) signals the pattern
- phrase: 'I know, I'll ...' opens each plan — functions participation, rhythm; effective: True — a joinable cue that marks each new scheme
- callback: every object from the failed plans floats in the medicine sea — functions memory, humor; effective: True — gathers the book's failures into the payoff picture

## Strengths

- a clear pattern of three attempts with a predictable, joinable failure beat
- failures are funny and land in the picture, letting the text stay to one word
- the child decides to try; the change is his
- the final imagined sea gathers every earlier plan
- very short sentences suited to a young listener

## Weaknesses

- the health message is stated at the end (all I had to do was drink my medicine)
- the bribe asks another child to drink his medicine, presented as a joke without comment
- the speaker of untagged lines depends on the bubbles; read aloud without pictures several lines are ambiguous
- medicine is shown as instantly transformative
